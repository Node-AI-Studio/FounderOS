# Hatch

Maker of the Restore smart alarm clock: sunrise-simulating light, white noise
and guided content, controlled through a companion app with a subscription
tier for the deeper content library. Hatch sells routine and ambience: a
single bedside object that replaces a phone alarm, a sound machine and a
meditation app.

Hatch's strength is breadth. One device does five jobs passably, which reads
as convenience to a buyer who has never tried a single-purpose therapy device.
Its weakness for our purposes is the app-and-account layer: Restore needs a
phone, a login and, for full functionality, a paid subscription tier.

Helight's counter-angle sits directly against that: no app, no account, no
subscription (see [[wiki/angle-bank]]), plus a specific, patented,
28-minute mechanism ([[products/the-28-minute-protocol]]) rather than a
general-purpose sunrise light. Where Hatch sells a routine, Helight sells a
protocol with a results promise attached. The comparison content in
[[campaigns/melatonin-quitters]] and the site's planned compare page
(see [[wiki/credibility-gaps-on-the-site]]) both name Hatch as the reference
point for "why not just get a sunrise lamp."
