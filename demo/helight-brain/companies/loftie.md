# Loftie

DTC alarm clock brand positioned around getting a phone out of the bedroom.
The Loftie clock combines a soft alarm, curated soundscapes, guided breathing
and short "wind-down" audio sessions, sold as a phone replacement more than a
sleep treatment.

Loftie's pitch is behavioural: the problem is late-night scrolling, and the
fix is a nicer object to reach for instead. That framing overlaps directly
with one of Helight's own audience lanes, late-night scrollers, in
[[wiki/audience-lanes]]. The difference in mechanism is total: Loftie offers
no light-based or physiological pathway, only substitution and routine.

Helight's counter-angle is mechanism over habit: a 630 nm light exposure with
a published research chain behind it ([[wiki/study-chain]]) against Loftie's
soundscape-and-willpower approach. The format that works best in this
comparison is the wind-down routine video from
[[wiki/format-playbook]], shot to show the Helight replacing the phone on the
nightstand the same way a Loftie clock would, but with a physiological
mechanism attached to the claim.

See [[wiki/competitor-frame]] for how this and the other three bedside
competitors are framed against each other.
