# Study Chain

The six-study chain that backs Helight's mechanism, in order, each detailed
in its own source page:

1. [[sources/berson-hattar-2002]]: Berson and Hattar's foundational discovery
   of intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells (ipRGCs), the
   photoreceptor class that lets light signal the circadian system.
2. [[sources/basketball-players-2012]]: a 2012 study on basketball players
   linking light exposure timing to sleep and performance outcomes.
3. [[sources/intensity-2017]]: a 2017 study on light intensity's role in the
   effect.
4. [[sources/sleep-onset-2018]]: a 2018 study on sleep onset specifically.
5. [[sources/sleep-inertia-2019]]: a 2019 study on sleep inertia, the grogginess
   on waking.
6. [[sources/strasbourg-2024]]: the most recent study, from Strasbourg in 2024.

This chain runs from basic circadian science (2002) toward increasingly
applied, sleep-specific findings (2018 to 2024), which is the right narrative
order for [[wiki/format-playbook]]'s physician-explainer format: start from
"why light affects sleep at all," then narrow to "why this specific protocol."
See [[wiki/evidence-base]] for how this sits with awards and press, and
[[wiki/claims-allowlist]] for what phrasing this evidence actually supports.
