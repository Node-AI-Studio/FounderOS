# Berson and Hattar (2002)

## What it says

Identified intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells (ipRGCs): a
class of retinal photoreceptor, separate from rods and cones, that signals
light and dark directly to the circadian system rather than to vision.
Established the biological pathway by which light exposure timing can shift
circadian rhythm independent of what a person consciously sees.

## Date

2002.

## Which claims it supports

The foundational science behind [[products/the-28-minute-protocol]]: the
mechanism-level claim that a specific light exposure, delivered at the right
time, has a physiological pathway to affect sleep onset. Cited at the head of
[[wiki/study-chain]] and [[wiki/evidence-base]], and is the first, most
defensible study to reference in a physician-explainer format
([[wiki/format-playbook]]) since it establishes mechanism before any
sleep-specific outcome claim.
